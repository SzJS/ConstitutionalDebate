# Minimal Deliberative Constitution

A placeholder standard, written to exercise the mechanism rather than to settle
anything. The constitution is a **slot**: swapping this file is the intended way
to run the same question under a different normative standard, and the text below
carries no more authority than any other candidate.

Provisions are numbered so that a debater can cite one by identifier and quote
its operative words, and so that a reader of the transcript can check the
citation against this file.

## §1 Justification

**§1.1** A decision must be justified by reasons that are available to those it
binds, stated in terms they could in principle contest.

**§1.2** A reason that cannot be stated publicly cannot support a decision.

**§1.3** Where the evidence for a claim is contested, the decision must say so
rather than assert the claim as settled.

## §2 Interests

**§2.1** The interests of everyone materially affected count, whether or not they
are represented in the debate.

**§2.2** Burdens falling on those least able to avoid or absorb them weigh more
heavily than equivalent burdens falling on those best able to.

**§2.3** A concentrated harm to a few is not outweighed merely by a diffuse
benefit to many; the benefit must be shown, not assumed.

## §3 Liberty and constraint

**§3.1** A restriction on what people may do requires a justification
proportionate to the restriction's severity and reach.

**§3.2** Where a less restrictive measure would achieve substantially the same
end, the more restrictive one is not justified.

## §4 Uncertainty

**§4.1** Where evidence is genuinely contested, prefer the decision that is
easier to reverse.

**§4.2** Speculative benefits weigh less than demonstrated ones. A prediction
about consequences is only as strong as the argument offered for it.

## §5 Consistency

**§5.1** Like cases are to be decided alike. A decision that cannot be stated as
a rule the decider would accept in relevantly similar cases is not justified.

**§5.2** No provision of this constitution may be read so as to render another
provision inoperative.
